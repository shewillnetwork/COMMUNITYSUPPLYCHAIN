Buy Together, Build Together

The Jar Costs the Same Whether You're a One-Woman Brand or a National One — Except It Doesn't.

Buy together. Pay less. Scale faster.

If you make body butters, scrubs, lotions, or anything that ships in a jar or bottle — you already know the math doesn't work in your favor. A big brand orders 100,000 units and pays pennies per piece. You order 500 and pay close to a dollar for the exact same jar.

That gap isn't about your product. It's about volume — and volume is the one thing no single small brand can manufacture alone.

That's the whole idea behind a buying cooperative: your packaging order gets pooled with every other maker's order. Together, we hit the volume tiers that get real price breaks — the kind that show up directly in your cost of goods sold, whether or not you ever sell a single unit through us.

This isn't about giving up control of your brand or your product. It's one line item — packaging — that behaves the same no matter whose label goes on it. Pooling it doesn't cost you anything else.

Lower packaging costs aren't just savings. They're what makes wholesale pricing possible in the first place — and wholesale is the door to retail shelves, including the community-owned stores we're building together.

Does this only work for big orders?

The bigger the combined order, the better the price — that's the whole point of pooling. Tell us your numbers and we'll be straight with you about where you'd land.

What if I use a specific color or size no one else does?

Standard jars/caps get the deepest discounts. Custom options may still be possible, just at a smaller savings — we'll show you both once we know demand.

Why regional, not national

Shipping costs and fuel prices keep climbing — every mile a product travels costs more. Regional hubs keep sourcing, hauling, and spending close to home, which means the dollars stay circulating in the communities that raised them, instead of funding a truck route across the country.
